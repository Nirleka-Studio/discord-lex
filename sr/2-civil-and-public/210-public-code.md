# SR 210 — The Public Code

## Preamble
Welcome to Nirleka Studios.

You might be reading this on our site, GitHub, or plain text, and you may notice we do not have a singular, long "rules" list compared to other Discord servers. Our rules here are named "laws".

We operate on a rule of law, not rule of vibes. And we have separate laws governing and outlining procedures and matters to keep things tidy and to let members know what we do and to let Administrators know how to do their zero-salary job.

You also have rights. Shocking, we know. Where other Discord servers operate like pure dictatorships, we operate on a dictatorship with standards. You may see your rights as outlined in our Server Charter[^1].

Here, the "Public Code" mainly serves as the general rules governing the horizontal relationship between members. The exact offences and their punishments are outlined by the Penal Code[^2], protecting members from arbitrary punishments.

---

Your data, including user IDs and messages, are logged and stored for moderation purposes. However, we do not retain sensitive personal information or illegal materials.

You may be punished for serious misconduct outside the Server as defined by Server laws.

If you do not agree with this, you may leave the Server.

## Part 1: Introduction

### Title 1: General Principles

#### Chapter 1: Application and Interpretation

##### Section 1: Principle of Good Faith

###### Art. 1 Acting in good faith
<sup>1</sup> Every person must act in good faith in the exercise of his or her rights and in the performance of his or her obligations.

<sup>2</sup> The manifest abuse of a right is not protected by law.

###### Art. 2 Presumption of honesty and good faith
<sup>1</sup> Unless proven otherwise, a person is presumed to be acting honestly and in good faith when required by law.

<sup>2</sup> This assumption does not apply if a person failed to act with the care and caution that the situation reasonably required.

##### Section 2: Applying and Interpreting the Law

###### Art. 3 Application of the law
<sup>1</sup> The law applies to any matter falling within the scope of its text, spirit, or purpose.

<sup>2</sup> In the absence of a provision, the judicial authority decides in accordance with customary law and, in the absence of customary law, in accordance with the rule that it would make as a legislator.

<sup>3</sup> In doing so, the judicial authority must consider established legal doctrine and precedent.

###### Art. 4 Administrative discretion
When a law gives an interpreting authority the discretion to make its own judgment, asks it to weigh the situation, or requires a good reason to act, the interpreting authority must decide fairly and reasonably based on the circumstances of the case.

#### Chapter 2: Rules of Evidence

##### Art. 5 Burden of proof
Any person who asserts a fact to establish a right has the burden of proving that fact, unless the law provides otherwise.

##### Art. 6 Proof by public document
Public and platform records are presumed to be true, unless their content is shown to be incorrect.

##### Art. 7 Acquiring evidence
<sup>1</sup> A person must use and assume a fact from sufficient evidence available to them or from sources they can lawfully obtain.

<sup>2</sup> The use of bad faith practices for acquiring evidence is prohibited, unless in circumstances allowed by law.

<sup>3</sup> Where no sufficient evidence exists, an assumption must be made that favours the person against whom the fact is asserted, unless the law or the circumstances require otherwise.

#### Chapter 3: Rules of Obligations and Duties

##### Art. 8 Invalid obligations
<sup>1</sup> An obligation is invalid if it is impossible, unlawful under Server or real-world law, or contrary to good morals.

<sup>2</sup> Every other obligation must be performed competently and with reasonable effort.

##### Art. 9 Reliance on fact
Where an obligation requires a person to be informed of a fact, that fact must be communicated clearly.

##### Art. 10 Availability
<sup>1</sup> Where an obligation is placed on a person, they are not required to act while they are reasonably unavailable to do so.

<sup>2</sup> A person is considered reasonably unavailable where, having regard to the circumstances, they cannot reasonably be expected to perform the obligation at the relevant time.

<sup>3</sup> Unavailability does not permanently excuse an obligation. Where the obligation remains relevant after the person becomes available, they must act within a reasonable time.

<sup>4</sup> Where an obligation concerns an ongoing violation or a matter requiring timely intervention, the availability of another person capable of fulfilling the same obligation may be taken into account.

#### Chapter 4: Rules of the Platform

##### Art. 11 Following the rules of the platform
<sup>1</sup> Every person involved must follow the platform's Terms of Service and Community Guidelines.

<sup>2</sup> Minor infractions that cause no harm, disruption, or security risk do not on their own warrant enforcement action, which is reserved for material violations.

## Part 2: Law of Persons

### Title 1: Natural Persons

#### Chapter 1: General Principles

##### Art. 12 Natural person
An account is presumed to be fully owned and controlled by one natural person unless proven otherwise.

##### Art. 13 Account responsibility
<sup>1</sup> A person is responsible for conduct carried out through their account unless there is sufficient reason to believe that the account was not under their control at the time.

<sup>2</sup> A claim that an account was compromised must be assessed on the circumstances and available evidence and must not, by itself, excuse a violation.

##### Art. 14 Extension of liability to controlled accounts
<sup>1</sup> Liability for a violation of a law attaches to the natural person responsible.

<sup>2</sup> Any administrative sanction, restriction, or obligation imposed on a person applies equally to all accounts owned, operated, or controlled by that person.

#### Chapter 2: Personality Rights

##### Art. 15 Protection of personality rights
<sup>1</sup> Every person is entitled to the protection of their personality, which comprises everything they need to take part in the community with dignity and safety, whether on or off the Server.

<sup>2</sup> An infringement is unlawful unless justified by the consent of the person concerned, by an overriding private or public interest, or by law.

##### Art. 16 Protection of identity, data, and creation
<sup>1</sup> Every person is entitled to the protection of the use and representation of their identity, personal data, and creation.

<sup>2</sup> An infringement is unlawful unless justified by the consent of the person concerned, by an overriding private or public interest, or by law.

##### Art. 17 Remedies
<sup>1</sup> A person whose personality is unlawfully infringed or threatened may petition a judicial authority against all those contributing to the infringement.

<sup>2</sup> The petitioner may request that the authority prohibit a threatened infringement, end an existing one, or declare it unlawful.

<sup>3</sup> The authority may also order any measure suited to remedy the infringement, including removal, correction, or the party's refraining from further contact.

<sup>4</sup> A person who cannot reasonably act for themselves may be represented by another.

#### Chapter 3: Age Requirements

##### Section 1: Age of Majority

###### Art. 18 Minority status
<sup>1</sup> A person is considered a minor if they are under the age of 18.

<sup>2</sup> Minority status may be established through self-admission, contextual evidence from communication, or formal verification.

<sup>3</sup> Where reasonable doubt exists regarding a person's age in matters concerning safety or explicit content, the person is presumed to be a minor until proven otherwise.

##### Section 2: Minimum Platform Age

###### Art. 19 Persons of minimum platform age
<sup>1</sup> A person is of minimum platform age if they are the age of 13 or older.

<sup>2</sup> Every person is presumed to be of minimum platform age until proven otherwise.

###### Art. 20 Persons below the minimum platform age
<sup>1</sup> A person who is under the minimum platform age lacks legal capacity to hold membership or exercise rights under this Code.

<sup>2</sup> Self-admission or credible evidence of being under the minimum platform age invalidates membership immediately.

### Title 2: Legal Entities

#### Chapter 1: General Principles

##### Art. 21 Legal entity
<sup>1</sup> A legal entity is any non-human entity, automated process, or external integration operating on or interacting with the Server through a technical interface.

<sup>2</sup> Legal entities do not possess personality rights, membership standing, or independent legal capacity under this Code unless expressly granted by law.

##### Art. 22 Prohibition of impersonation
A legal entity must not be used to impersonate another natural person or mislead others into assuming it is a natural person.

##### Art. 23 Authorisation and admission
<sup>1</sup> No legal entity may be introduced to, integrated with, or operated within the Server without the prior authorisation of a competent administrative authority.

<sup>2</sup> Authorisation is granted for a specific functional purpose and may be modified, suspended, or revoked at any time without prior notice.

##### Art. 24 Attribution of conduct and liability
<sup>1</sup> A legal entity cannot bear personal culpability. All actions made by a legal entity are legally attributed to the natural person who owns, deploys, configures, or controls it.

<sup>2</sup> Where a legal entity is operated on behalf of the Server or its administration, liability for malfunction or unintended harm is governed by the rules on administrative acts, provided the operator acted in good faith and with reasonable care.

#### Chapter 2: Categories of Legal Entities

##### Section 1: Persistent Entities

###### Art. 25 Definition and scope
A persistent entity is a legal entity possessing a distinct identity, profile, or continuous presence within the Server capable of receiving, processing, or responding to interactions over time.

###### Art. 26 Scope of permissions
<sup>1</sup> A persistent entity may only be granted the minimum permissions strictly necessary to perform its authorised function.

<sup>2</sup> A persistent entity must not be used to circumvent restrictions or obligations placed upon its operator or any other person.

##### Section 2: Unidirectional Interfaces

###### Art. 27 Definition and scope
A unidirectional interface is a technical endpoint or automated conduit that transmits information or executes predefined actions into the Server from an external source without possessing an independent member presence.

## Part 3: Conduct of Persons

### Title 1: General Principles

#### Chapter 1: Dignity

##### Art. 28 Human dignity
Every person must respect the human dignity of another.

#### Chapter 2: Responsibility and Culpability

##### Art. 29 Responsibility
<sup>1</sup> Every person is responsible for conduct on and outside the Server.

<sup>2</sup> This includes what can be accessed by other members through their presence in the Server.

<sup>3</sup> This also includes, in some circumstances as defined by law, what a person associates with.

#### Chapter 3: The Community

##### Art. 30 Consideration for the community
<sup>1</sup> A person must not use the Server in a way that unreasonably burdens others' use of it or disrupts its ongoing life.

<sup>2</sup> A person must not impose on others content, volume, or promotion they have not agreed to receive and which the setting does not reasonably call for.

#### Chapter 4: Use of Server Facilities and Systems

##### Art. 31 In general
Every person must use Server facilities, systems, and processes as intended and in good faith.

### Title 2: Contents

#### Chapter 1: General Principles

###### Art. 32 Appropriateness of content
All content must be appropriate in the context and circumstance that it is placed and to the persons in that circumstance.

###### Art. 33 Restriction of access
All content must have its access restricted in a way the content reasonably requires so that it cannot be accessed by persons who have not consented or for whom it is inappropriate.

#### Chapter 2: Restricted Contents

###### Art. 34 Other content
Content is otherwise prohibited if it meets the special requirements that the following articles define.

###### Art. 35 Sensitive content
<sup>1</sup> Sensitive content is content of a sexual, violent, or otherwise disturbing character that a reasonable person would expect to offend or distress others, or to be unsuitable for minors.

<sup>2</sup> Whether content is sensitive, and how it is treated, is determined by its function, context, and likely effect on those who encounter it, not by its subject alone.

###### Art. 36 Prohibited content
<sup>1</sup> Sensitive content is prohibited where its predominant function or effect is:
a. sexual arousal or gratification; or
b. to shock, degrade, or glorify real suffering or harm.

<sup>2</sup> Content that sexualises a minor is always prohibited, whatever its stated purpose.

###### Art. 37 Restricted content
<sup>1</sup> Sensitive content that serves a genuine educational, medical, artistic, journalistic, or comparable purpose, and is not prohibited under Article 36, may be shared only:
a. in a designated facility, where its character is principally sexual or intimate; or
b. elsewhere, concealed and accompanied by a notice sufficient for others to decide whether to view it, where its character is principally disturbing.

<sup>2</sup> Where it is doubtful whether content is prohibited or restricted, it is treated as restricted.

###### Art. 38 Content outside scope
Content is not sensitive where, in the context, a reasonable person would not be offended or distressed by it, in particular where it is crude or childish in nature, or appears incidentally and briefly within other material and is concealed or removed upon request.

###### Art. 39 Presentation
Content does not lose its character because it is presented as humour, fiction, or stylisation, or is partially concealed or reached indirectly, where a reasonable person would still perceive that character.

#### Chapter 3: Other Prohibited Contents

##### Art. 40 Prohibition of destructive and illegal materials
<sup>1</sup> Promoting, facilitating, or providing actionable instructions for illegal acts or materials that cause real-world harm to life, infrastructure, or nature is prohibited.

<sup>2</sup> Where a material serves dual or multipurpose functions, its display, promotion, or distribution is permitted only when strictly contextualised by its lawful utilisation and balanced by good faith adherence to applicable regulations and safety standards.

##### Art. 41 Protection of devices and data
<sup>1</sup> A person must not create, distribute, or facilitate any material intended or likely to compromise the security, operation, or access controls of another person's system or data.

<sup>2</sup> Measures taken for system security, authorised testing, education, or reporting are exempt.

### Title 3: Duties and Obligations of Members

#### Chapter 1: Representation and Honesty

##### Art. 42 Representation of facts
<sup>1</sup> Every member must represent a factual claim truthfully.

<sup>2</sup> Every member must make a genuine effort to verify the truthfulness of a claim before representing it, as the matter reasonably requires.

<sup>3</sup> This does not apply to representations that a reasonable person would recognise as humorous, inaccurate, or untrue.

##### Art. 43 Reliance on claims
Every member must make a genuine effort to verify the truthfulness of a claim before committing an act based on that claim.

#### Chapter 2: Affirmative Duties

##### Art. 44 Duty to report
Every person has the duty to report unlawful actions or materials under real-world or Server laws to the appropriate competent authorities.

##### Art. 45 Duty to help
Every person has the duty to direct a person who expresses or exhibits signs of a health, medical, or mental well-being concern to available institutional help and approach the person with respect.

##### Art. 46 Duty to touch grass
<sup>1</sup> Every person has an ongoing duty to disengage periodically from the Server and the global digital infrastructure.

<sup>2</sup> Fulfilment of this duty requires active participation in real-world maintenance, including the obligation to:
a. be off the Server and the internet;
b. commit to house chores;
c. witness nature and the outside world;
d. socialise with real persons in the outside world;
e. maintain hygiene;
f. contribute to society; and
g. maintain physical and mental health.

<sup>3</sup> Persistent failure may be treated as a chronic status infraction, warranting recommendations to disconnect, seek fresh air, or re-evaluate life priorities.
