# SR 410 — Administration Codes Compilations
*(Administration Codes)*

## Title 1: General Provisions

### Chapter 1: Fundamentals

##### Art. 1 General terms
Unless the law states otherwise, any term generally defined in other laws defines the term used in this Code.

##### Art. 2 Application
This Code applies to circumstances it defines, alongside the terms used as defined by laws in force at the time.

##### Art. 3 Terms and definitions
<sup>1</sup> *"Illegal"* means prohibited by applicable real-world laws.

##### Art. 4 Authorities and obligations
Unless the law states otherwise, this Code and obligations applies to Administrators and enforcing authorities.

## Title 2: Specific Provisions

### Chapter 1: Day-to-Day Operations

#### Section 1: Deletions

##### Art. 5 Deletion of prohibited content
Any prohibited content must be deleted immediately.

##### Art. 6 Deletion of sensitive or illegal content
<sup>1</sup> Any personally identifiable information of a person without their consent must be deleted immediately, including from records.

<sup>2</sup> Any illegal content must be deleted immediately, including from records.

##### Art. 7 Deletion of endangering content
Any content that might endanger a person must be deleted immediately, including from records.

#### Section 2: Suspicious Activities

##### Art. 8 Suspicious accounts
Any account exhibiting suspicious activities as defined in Annex 1 must be banned immediately.

##### Art. 9 Compromised accounts
<sup>1</sup> Any account exhibiting compromised or suspicious activities as defined in Annex 1 must be banned immediately.

<sup>2</sup> If the owner of the account gains back control of the account, they must be unbanned and may be invited to the Server.

## Annex 1
(Art. 8)

### Indicators of Suspicious Account Activity

#### 1 Principle
An account is deemed to exhibit suspicious activity if it meets one or more of the observable behavioural indicators set out in Section 2.

#### 2 Indicators

##### 2.1 Profile and Account Anomalies
An account exhibits suspicious activity if:
a. the account age at the time of joining is less than the minimum threshold established by the server configuration;
b. the account profile uses known impersonation assets, including names or avatars belonging to server staff, official bots, or Discord representatives;
c. the account exhibits avatar, username, or profile bio patterns associated with automated self-bots or mass-creation scripts.

##### 2.2 Messaging and Interaction Anomalies
An account exhibits suspicious activity if:
a. it dispatches identical or near-identical messages across multiple channels or via unsolicited Direct Messages at a frequency exceeding human capability (mass spamming);
b. it includes unauthorised external links, phishing domains, IP-logging tools, or file attachments classified as malicious;
c. it executes mass user mentions (e.g., `@everyone`, `@here`, or mass role tags) without administrative authorisation;
d. it systematically sends invite links to external Discord servers in violation of local server rules.

##### 2.3 Server and Guild Exploitation (Raiding)
An account exhibits suspicious activity if:
a. it joins the server as part of a synchronised, high-volume influx of accounts within a designated timeframe (raid participant);
b. it systematically evades previous moderation actions, including using alternate accounts to circumvent bans or mutes;
c. it uses self-bots, automated account scripts, or user-bots to bypass human interaction requirements.

#### 3 Evidence and Operational Thresholds
<sup>1</sup> Administrators and automated enforcement tools must evaluate the indicators in Section 2 using server Audit Logs, AutoMod flags, and bot event logs.

<sup>2</sup> The exact numerical limits (e.g., maximum messages per second, account age minimums) is configured in the administrative settings of the server moderation tools.
