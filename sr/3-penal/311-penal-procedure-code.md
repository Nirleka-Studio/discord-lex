# SR 311 — Penal Procedure Code
*(ProNaCo, PPC)*

The Director of Nirleka Studios,

based on Chapter 4 of the Server Charter (SR 101),

decrees:

## Chapter 1: Scope of Application and Principles

### Art. 1 Scope of Application
<sup>1</sup> This Code regulates the prosecution and adjudication by members of Administration for offences under Server laws.

<sup>2</sup> The procedural regulations contained in other Server laws are reserved.

### Art. 2 Administration of penalties and measures
<sup>1</sup> The administration of penalties and measures is the responsibility solely of the authorities specified by law.

<sup>2</sup> Penal proceedings may be conducted and concluded only in the forms provided for by law.

## Chapter 2: Principles of Penal Procedure Laws

### Art. 3 Independence
Enforcing and judicial authorities are independent in applying the law and bound solely by the law.

### Art. 4 Principle of expeditiousness
<sup>1</sup> The Administration must start penal proceedings immediately and conclude them without unjustified delay.

<sup>2</sup> Where an accused is unavailable, penal proceedings must still continue, including:
a. the logging of the violation; and
b. any relevant procedures outlined by law.

### Art. 5 Obligation to act
Enforcing authorities are under the obligation to act according to law upon a violation of law.

### Art. 6 Principle of no judgment without a charge
An offence may only be judicially assessed if an enforcing authority has brought a related charge against a specific person in the competent judicial authority based on precisely described circumstances.

### Art. 7 Prohibition of double jeopardy
No person who has been convicted or acquitted in the Server by a final legally binding judgment may be prosecuted again for the same offence.

### Art. 8 Presumption of innocence and assessment of evidence
<sup>1</sup> Every person is presumed to be innocent until they have been convicted in a judgment that is final and legally binding.

<sup>2</sup> The judicial authority must be free to interpret the evidence in accordance with the views that it forms over the entire proceedings.

<sup>3</sup> Where there is insurmountable doubt whether the factual requirements of alleged offence have been fulfilled, the judicial authority shall proceed on the assumption that the circumstances more favourable to the accused occurred.

### Art. 9 Evidence
The enforcing authority bears the burden of establishing that a violation occurred.

### Art. 10 Case records
<sup>1</sup> Every judicial proceeding must receive a unique case identifier.

<sup>2</sup> The case record must contain, at minimum:
a. the case identifier;
b. the parties involved;
c. the challenged action or legal question;
d. the applicable law;
e. the material evidence;
f. the final decision; and
g. the reasoning supporting the decision.

<sup>3</sup> Judicial records must be maintained in an auditable format.

### Art. 11 Right to notice
A person must be informed what law and specific, relevant provisions they have been charged, acquitted, and punished for.

### Art. 12 Conflict of interest
Judicial authorities must not decide on a case in which they are personally involved.

## Chapter 3: The Authorities

#### Art. 13 Knowledge of the law
Administrators, including judicial and enforcing authorities, must be knowledgeable about the law

### Section 1: The Enforcing Authorities

#### Art. 14 The enforcing authorities
The enforcing authorities are:
a. members of the Administration's Executive Branch as defined in Section 2 of the Server Charter; and
b. their appointed staff.

### Section 2: The Judicial Authorities

#### Art. 15 The judicial authorities
<sup>1</sup> Judicial authority rests with the Secretary.

<sup>2</sup> Where the Secretary is not available or is a named party in a dispute, the Director may hold judicial authority.

#### Art. 16 Rule of necessity
Where both the Secretary and the Director are conflicted in a single matter, and no impartial authority is lawfully able to be assigned, the Director must issue a binding administrative decision to prevent a deadlock.

## Chapter 4: Penal Procedures

### Section 1: Procedures for Member Violations

#### Art. 17 Regular member
A regular member is a Server member holding no position in the Administration.

#### Art. 18 No judicial procedures
If a regular member violates a law, unless a law says otherwise, judicial procedures are not required.

#### Art. 19 Right of appeal
<sup>1</sup> Judicial proceedings are required if the affected member requests an appeal.

<sup>2</sup> Where a member is still serving their duration of punishment, proceedings must be conducted off-Server.

#### Art. 20 Enforcement
<sup>1</sup> Upon a regular member violating a law, enforcing authorities must immediately:
a. impose a measure outlined by law; and
b. document relevant administrative and moderation actions.

### Section 2: Procedures for Violations by the Administration

#### Art. 21 Suspension
<sup>1</sup> A suspension removes administrative permissions normally granted to an Administrator for their position, and they may not exercise any administrative powers during the suspension period.

<sup>2</sup> If substantial evidence of a grave violation of law is reported to a Secretary, or other superior Administrators, the accused Administrator must be suspended.

<sup>3</sup> Judicial authorities must not impose a ban as a measure against an Administrator if the applicable law does not explicitly state such measure.

#### Art. 22 Violations from administrators
<sup>1</sup> Judicial proceedings are required for Administrators who committed violations of law.

<sup>2</sup> Judicial authorities have broad discretion to raise the measure for violations committed by Administrators based on their seniority.

## Chapter 5: Appeals

### Art. 23 Appeals
<sup>1</sup> A member affected by a moderation or administrative action may appeal that action to judicial authorities.

<sup>2</sup> An appeal may challenge:
a. whether the alleged conduct occurred;
b. whether the conduct constituted a violation of law;
c. whether the cited law was correctly interpreted or applied;
d. whether the penalty was lawful or proportionate; or
e. whether the procedure followed by Administration was lawful.

### Art. 24 Filing an appeal
<sup>1</sup> An appeal must identify:
a. the appellant;
b. the administrative action being challenged;
c. the law or decision concerned, where known;
d. the grounds of appeal; and
e. any evidence or information relied upon.

<sup>2</sup> An appeal must be concise and may be submitted in any reasonable format determined by the Secretary.

<sup>3</sup> Minor defects in an appeal must not result in its dismissal where the substance of the complaint can reasonably be understood.

## Chapter 6: Triage and Informal Resolution

### Art. 25 Screening and classification
<sup>1</sup> Upon receiving an appeal, the judicial authority must classify the matter as either:
a. a Summary Review, for minor infractions, clear errors, or routine moderation; or
b. a Formal Proceeding, for severe penalties, staff violations, or complex factual disputes.

<sup>2</sup> An appeal must be filed within 14 days of the penal measure being imposed. Late filings are dismissed as inadmissible unless extraordinary circumstances exist.

### Art. 26 Summary review procedure
<sup>1</sup> In a Summary Review, the judicial authority must examine the record provided by the appellant and the enforcing authority without opening formal hearings.

<sup>2</sup> If the judicial authority finds that a measure was unlawful, unsupported by evidence, or disproportionate, they must issue a summary order directing the enforcing authority to modify, commute, or immediately lift the measure.

<sup>3</sup> Enforcing authorities are bound to execute summary orders without delay.

## Chapter 7: Formal judicial proceedings

### Art. 27 Opening of proceedings
<sup>1</sup> Where a matter is designated for Formal Proceeding, the judicial authority must establish a secure, dedicated channel or ticket off-Server for the exchange of arguments and evidence.

<sup>2</sup> The filing of an appeal does not suspend the execution of the penalty unless the judicial authority grants a temporary stay.

### Art. 28 Evidence and submissions
<sup>1</sup> The enforcing authority has 48 hours to submit all logs, screenshots, and statement of facts establishing the violation.

<sup>2</sup> If the enforcing authority fails to submit sufficient evidence within the prescribed timeframe, the judicial authority must rule in favour of the appellant and dismiss the charges.

<sup>3</sup> The appellant must be given a single, structured opportunity to review the enforcing authority's evidence and submit a final rebuttal.

### Art. 29 Adjudication and ruling
<sup>1</sup> The judicial authority must issue a written judgment within 7 days of opening the proceeding.

<sup>2</sup> The judgment must contain:
a. a summary of the facts;
b. the relevant legal provisions applied;
c. the verdict (Upheld, Modified, or Overturned); and
d. the remedy or order, if applicable.

<sup>3</sup> Rulings issued by the competent judicial authority are final and binding on all members and Administration.
