# SR Numbering Standard

## But What is "SR"?

_SR_ stands for "Systematische Rechtssammlung" (Systematic Collection of Federal Law) which is the name of Switzerland's official government codification system.

Why use it? Because it is already being used for a long time and it is practical. Do not ask any more questions.

### Art. 1 Chapters
<sup>1</sup> `SR 1XX` Constitutional.\
<sup>2</sup> `SR 2XX` Civil and public.\
<sup>3</sup> `SR 3XX` Penal.\
<sup>4</sup> `SR 4XX` Administration.\
<sup>5</sup> `SR 5XX` Culture.\
<sup>6</sup> `SR 6XX` Economy.

<sup>7</sup> Chapters are fixed once assigned. A chapter may not be renumbered, merged, or reassigned to a different domain after its first sub-chapter is filed.

### Art. 2 Sub-chapters
<sup>1</sup> Sub-chapters under a major chapter start at `X10` and increment in steps of five: `210, 215, 220, 225...`. Do not fill sequentially (`210, 211, 212...`), the gaps are reserved for topics not yet anticipated.

<sup>2</sup> The Server Charter (`SR 100`) is exempted from paragraph 1 and remains at `100`.

<sup>3</sup> If every reserved gap between two existing subchapters is exhausted, a new subchapter number must be opened elsewhere in the chapter rather than subdividing an existing one further (see Art. 3 §3).

### Art. 3 Decimal suffix
<sup>1</sup> A sub-chapter's foundational document, the base Code for that topic, is filed at the bare sub-chapter number, with no decimal suffix. Example: `SR 410` is the Administration Code.

<sup>2</sup> Documents implementing, amending, or clarifying that base Code are filed as `SUBCHAPTER.NN`, numbered in the order they are adopted,not by theme. Example: `SR 410.01` defines how to document punishments as mentioned in the Administration Code; `SR 410.02` would be whatever is adopted next under that same Code, regardless of subject.

<sup>3</sup> **A code may contain at most one decimal group.** If an implementing document itself grows complex enough to need further subdivision, it must be promoted to its own new sub-chapter number under Art. 2, not extended with an additional digit or decimal group (e.g. `410.01.1` or `410.011` are prohibited forms).

<sup>4</sup> A decimal-suffixed document may not itself have an SR number lower than its parent's; adoption order must strictly increase.

### Art. 4 Numbering integrity
<sup>1</sup> Once assigned, an SR number is never reused, even after the document it identifies is repealed. A repealed number is retired permanently and noted as such in the master index.

<sup>2</sup> Every chapter's Art. 2 gap allocation and every sub-chapter's current highest-used decimal suffix must be tracked in a single master index, updated at the time of filing, this index is the sole authoritative source for "what number is free next."
